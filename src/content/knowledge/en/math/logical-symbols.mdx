# Logical symbols

These symbols express mathematical statements precisely.

| Symbol | Read as | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\in$ | belongs to | $5\in\mathbb N$ |
| $\notin$ | does not belong to | $-2\notin\mathbb N$ |
| $\subset$ | is a subset of | $\mathbb N\subset\mathbb Z$ |
| $\not\subset$ | is not a subset of | $\mathbb R\not\subset\mathbb Q$ |
| $\forall$ | for every | $\forall n\in\mathbb N$ |
| $\exists$ | there exists | $\exists x\in\mathbb R$ |
| $\nexists$ | there does not exist | $\nexists x\in\mathbb R:x^2=-1$ |
| $\Rightarrow$ | implies | $x>2\Rightarrow x>1$ |
| $\Leftrightarrow$ | if and only if | $x^2=4\Leftrightarrow x=\pm2$ |
| $\ne$ | is not equal to | $2\ne3$ |
| $\leq$ | is less than or equal to | $x\leq5$ |
| $\geq$ | is greater than or equal to | $x\geq0$ |

<Grid>
  <Example title="Reading a sequence definition">
    $$\forall n\in\mathbb N,\qquad u_n=2n+1$$
    reads: “For every natural number $n$, the term at index $n$ equals
    $2n+1$.” Here, $n$ is the index and $u_n$ is the corresponding term.
  </Example>

  <Definition title="Implication">
    $P\Rightarrow Q$ says that if $P$ is true, then $Q$ is true. It does not
    guarantee that $Q\Rightarrow P$.
  </Definition>

  <Definition title="Equivalence">
    $P\Leftrightarrow Q$ means that both implications $P\Rightarrow Q$ and
    $Q\Rightarrow P$ are true.
  </Definition>

  <CommonMistake>
    $\Rightarrow$ and $\Leftrightarrow$ are not interchangeable. For example,
    $x=2\Rightarrow x^2=4$, but $x^2=4$ does not imply $x=2$ because $x$ may
    also equal $-2$.
  </CommonMistake>
</Grid>

<Summary>
  The four priorities are: the chain of number sets, $x\in\mathbb R$,
  $\forall$ (“for every”), and $\exists$ (“there exists”).
</Summary>
