### The two easy-to-confuse ones

**Element → set:**

5∈N5\boxed{\in}\mathbb N

> **5 belongs to N.**

**Set → set:**

N⊂Z\mathbb N\boxed{\subset}\mathbb Z

> **N is included in Z.**

So:

number ∈ set\boxed{\text{number }\in\text{ set}} set ⊂ set\boxed{\text{set }\subset\text{ set}}

### For sequences

You'll see things such as:

∀n∈N,un=2n+1\forall n\in\mathbb N,\quad u_n=2n+1

Read:

**FR:** _Pour tout nn appartenant à N\mathbb N, un=2n+1u_n=2n+1._

**EN:** _For every natural number nn, un=2n+1u_n=2n+1._

Here:

- nn = **indice** / index
    
- unu_n = **terme d'indice nn** / term with index nn
    
- ∀\forall = every
    
- ∈\in = belongs to
    
- N\mathbb N = natural numbers
    

So the scary-looking

∀n∈N\forall n\in\mathbb N

really just means:

for every n=0,1,2,3,…\boxed{\text{for every }n=0,1,2,3,\ldots}

assuming your course defines 0∈N0\in\mathbb N.
